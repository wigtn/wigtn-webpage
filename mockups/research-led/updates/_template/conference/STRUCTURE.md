# Conference / workshop report

For a venue we attended or presented at: a paper, a demo booth, an invited
talk, a poster. `kind: "event"`, `newsTopic: "announcement"`.

Reference implementation: [`../../acl-2026-san-diego/index.ts`](../../acl-2026-san-diego/index.ts).

## Sections, in the order to write them

1. **Lede** — what, where, when, in one paragraph. Name the venue and the
   track. "A poster in a hallway" and "a demo booth running a live phone line
   for three days" are different stories, and the reader should know which one
   they are getting.
2. **Arriving** — the scale of the thing, before you were part of it. One or
   two photos of the venue earn their place here and nowhere else.
3. **What we brought** — the work itself, with the numbers that are already
   published. This is the only section where a reader who skipped the paper
   catches up.
4. **What happened** — the floor, the booth, the session. Photos belong here.
5. **What people asked** — the interactions worth reporting, and the questions
   you did not prepare for. The unprepared ones are the section.
6. **Side sessions** — the talks, posters, and workshops around the main event,
   including the ones that were not yours.
7. **What changes because of this** — decisions, as a list.
8. **Thanks to** — organizers and chairs by published role, then the people who
   came back a second time.

## The section that carries the post

Section 7. It is the one that gets skipped and the one readers want.

Write decisions, not reflections. "It was inspiring" tells nobody anything;
"speech recognition is 97.1% of the latency on that path, so optimization goes
there" does. Every item should name the evidence that forced it — usually a
number from the paper or a question that came up more than once at the booth.

If you came back with three decisions instead of six, write three. A short list
of real ones reads as a team that was paying attention. A padded list reads as
a team that was not.

## Photo budget

A conference post can carry a lot of photos — the ACL report runs twenty — but
they have to be doing different jobs: the venue, the work on screen, the crowd,
the talk, the social event. Five photos of the same booth from five angles is
one photo.

Never close on a team shot that is already the cover. Showing the same frame
twice in one scroll reads as a mistake.
